The best world clock for Windows, feature by feature

Open World Clock keeps multiple time zones on your desktop, one card for each city you care about. Each card follows its city’s real sunrise and sunset, and one scroll converts every clock at once. This is everything in version 1.3.1, and all of it is free, with no strings attached.

Who’s awake, at a glance

Every city gets a card with a big clock. The card’s sky comes from that city’s sun, not from a fixed hour, so one look tells you who’s at their desk and who’s asleep.

  • One card per city

    Any IANA time zone works, including half-hour and 45-minute offsets. Each card shows the time, the date, the UTC offset and how far it is from your own clock.

  • Real sunrise and sunset

    Cards turn to night at the actual sunset, worked out on your PC from each city’s coordinates. Hover a card to see today’s sunrise and sunset.

  • Seven phases of the day

    Night, Dawn, Morning, Midday, Afternoon, Golden and Dusk, read from the height of the sun, with sky colors to match.

  • Clock-change notes

    In the week before daylight saving time starts or ends, the card says so, for example “Clocks +1h tomorrow”.

  • Search that finds it

    Look a city up by name, country, abbreviation (EST, CET) or offset (UTC-5, +3). Accents are optional, so Sao Paulo finds São Paulo.

  • Your own labels

    Rename any card. Show America/Los_Angeles as “San Francisco”, or name a card after the teammate who lives there.

  • 12 or 24 hours

    Pick your clock format and whether to show seconds.

Convert any time

The converter sits in the top bar. Type a time and pick a city, or just scroll over a card. Every clock follows.

  • Type it your way

    9, 9:30, 930, 9.30, 3pm or 15h30. Pick the city the time belongs to and every card shows the matching time.

  • Or scrub

    Turn the mouse wheel over any card, or drag along its day line, to move every clock in 15-minute steps. Back to now puts them all back.

  • +1 day and -1 day

    When the date is different somewhere, that card says so.

  • Likely asleep

    While you convert, cities between 22:00 and 07:00 dim and get a moon.

  • Other days

    Today, Tomorrow or one of the next five days by name, counted from the source city’s calendar.

  • Copy times

    One click copies every converted time, ready to paste into a chat or an invite. The copy menu also has Copy for Discord, Copy in UTC and Save calendar invite.

Plan meetings across time zones

Open the planner and each city gets a 24-hour row. Working hours are blue, nights are dark, and the hours when everyone is at work are outlined.

  • Working hours per city

    Every city starts at 09:00 to 18:00, Monday to Friday. Click its name to change the hours or the days.

  • Night shifts

    Set an end time earlier than the start, like 22:00 to 06:00, and the shift runs into the next morning.

  • A summary you can read

    The top line says it plainly: “4 h overlap · 14:00 to 18:00 (Lisbon)”, or “No overlap”.

  • Best hours when nobody overlaps

    With no shared hour, the planner outlines the hours when the most cities are at work, for example “Best: 12:00 to 13:00 (New York), 4 of 5 working”. Its tooltip names who is outside their hours.

  • Pick an hour, convert everything

    Click an hour in the grid and every clock jumps to it. Drag across hours, or hold Shift and use the arrow keys, to pick a longer slot, then copy it or save it as a calendar invite.

The world map

Switch to the map to see where it’s day and night right now, and where each of your cities sits.

  • Day and night, live

    Night is shaded with a soft twilight edge, and it moves as the minutes pass.

  • Where the sun is

    A small sun marks the spot where it’s directly overhead right now.

  • Your cities, pinned

    Each city on your list gets a pin with its name and time. Click one to convert from it.

  • Follows the converter

    Scrub to another time and the night moves with you.

Made for your desk

A small window that stays out of the way until you need it.

  • Three layouts

    Strip, compact or vertical, switched from the top bar. Each one remembers its own size.

  • Stays on top, if you want

    Pin it above every window, or let it sit with the rest.

  • Frameless and translucent

    Set the background opacity to taste. On Windows 11 it gets Snap and the native acrylic backdrop.

  • Always where you left it

    A tray icon, optional launch at login, and it reopens in the same place, on any monitor.

Themes and languages

  • Light, dark or Windows

    Follow the Windows setting, or pick one.

  • Three languages

    English, Portuguese and Spanish, taken from your Windows display language or set by hand. City and country names are translated too.

  • Motion that listens

    Animations switch off when Windows animation effects are off.

  • 12 or 24 hours, from Windows

    On first run the clock follows your Windows Region format: 12-hour for English (United States), 24-hour for Portuguese (Brazil). Change it any time in Settings.

  • Zoom to 200%

    Press Ctrl + + and Ctrl + -, or hold Ctrl and turn the mouse wheel, to zoom from 100% to 200%. Ctrl + 0 resets it. The window grows with it, so the layout keeps its shape, and the zoom is remembered.

Private, free and open source

  • Nothing leaves your PC

    No account, no ads, no telemetry. The app makes no network requests of its own, so it works offline.

  • One settings file

    Your cities, labels, working hours and preferences live in %APPDATA%\Open World Clock.

  • MIT licensed

    Free, with no in-app purchases. The source is on GitHub for anyone to read.

Read the privacy policy

Left out on purpose

A world clock that does one job well stays quick and light.

  • Weather. It would need the internet, and the app works offline.
  • Alarms, timers and a stopwatch. The Clock app that comes with Windows already does these well.
  • Other platforms, for now. It’s Windows only. An iPhone version with widgets is in the works, with no date yet.
