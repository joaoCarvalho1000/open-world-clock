What World Time Buddy does well

The hour grid is a great idea, and World Time Buddy does it well: a row of hours for each city, lined up, so the hour that suits everyone jumps out. It works in any browser, phones included, and you can send a colleague a link to exactly what you see.

If you plan meetings from your phone, or on a computer where you can’t install anything, it’s a good choice. On a PC you can’t install software on, you can also try Open World Clock live in the browser.

Why a Windows app for the same job

If you work with people in other time zones every day, a browser tab is one more thing to find, reload and sign in to. Open World Clock keeps your cities on the desktop, above every window if you pin it, and it answers “what time is it there?” without opening anything.

It runs on your PC, so it works offline, loads nothing from the internet and has no ads, no account and no telemetry. The code is open, MIT licensed, on GitHub.

The hour grid, with working hours

The meeting planner lines up a 24-hour row for each of your cities and outlines the hours when everyone is at work.

  • Working hours per city

    Each city keeps its own start, end and working days, so an office that works Sunday to Thursday or a night shift fits in.

  • The overlap, summed up

    Shared working hours are outlined, with a line like “2 h overlap · 16:00 to 18:00 (Lisbon)”.

  • When nothing fits

    It outlines the hours when the most cities are at work, for example “Best: 12:00 to 13:00 (New York), 4 of 5 working”.

  • Daylight saving, per day

    Each day uses its own rules, so the weeks when Europe and the US change clocks on different dates come out right.

How the meeting planner works

Converting a time

Type a time such as 3pm, 9:30 or 15h30, pick the city it belongs to, and every clock shows the matching time, with +1 day where the date changes. Or turn the mouse wheel over any card to move every city in 15-minute steps. Copy times puts the whole list on the clipboard, ready to paste into an invite. More about the time zone converter.

Which one should you use?

  • On your phone, or on a computer you can’t install anything on: Both work in the browser. Open World Clock also offers a free, offline Windows app.
  • A link to send someone: Both can share a link. Open World Clock can also copy times and export a calendar event.
  • Your cities in view all day on a Windows PC, and meetings across them, offline and without ads: Open World Clock.
