Find a meeting time in four steps

  1. Add the cities. Type each one in the Add city box. Search works by name, country, abbreviation or UTC offset.
  2. Open the planner. Select the planner button in the top bar. Each city gets a 24-hour row.
  3. Set each city’s hours. Click a city’s name to set its working hours and days. Every city starts at 09:00 to 18:00, Monday to Friday.
  4. Pick an hour. Click an hour inside the outlined overlap. Every clock converts to it, and Copy times puts the result on your clipboard.

How to read the grid

  • Columns are the source city’s hours

    The 24 columns are the hours of the chosen day in the converter’s city. Each row shows what time that is locally.

  • Three kinds of hour

    Blue is working time for the whole hour, dark is night (22:00 to 07:00 local), plain is off hours.

  • The overlap

    Hours where every city is working are outlined, and the top line sums them up. A second block adds “+1 more”; none at all says “No overlap”. When no hour works for everyone, the planner outlines the hours when the most cities are at work and names who is outside their hours, for example “Best: 12:00 to 13:00 (New York), 4 of 5 working”.

  • Now, and the rest of the week

    A thin line marks the current time. The date shortcuts (Today, Tomorrow and the next five days) show the days ahead.

  • Keyboard

    Arrow keys move through hours and cities; Home and End jump to the first and last hour.

  • Your time zone, your format

    Hours follow your 12 or 24-hour setting, and cities keep the labels you gave them.

Working hours and days, per city

Teams don’t all work 9 to 6, Monday to Friday. Each city keeps its own start, end and working days, so an office in Riyadh can work Sunday to Thursday and a support desk in Manila can work nights. Reset puts a city back to the default. The hours are saved with your settings, on your PC.

Night shifts

Set an end time earlier than the start, say 22:00 to 06:00, and the editor notes “Ends the next day”. The whole shift belongs to the day it starts: a shift that starts on Friday evening runs until Saturday morning.

Example: a team in Manila working 22:00 to 06:00, Monday to Friday, and an office in New York on 09:00 to 18:00. The planner shows 8 h overlap · 10:00 to 18:00 (New York), because Manila’s night is New York’s working day.

When the clocks change

Clocks change on different dates around the world, and the planner uses each day’s own rules. London and New York share 4 working hours on 21 October 2026, but 5 on 28 October, because Europe has already turned its clocks back and the US waits until 1 November. The same thing happens for two weeks in March. Worth a look before you set up a recurring meeting.
