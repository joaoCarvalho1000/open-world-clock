A time zone converter that lives on your desktop

Type a time, pick the city it belongs to, and every clock on your desktop shows the matching time. There’s no tab to open and no connection needed, and it gets daylight saving right.

Try it here

Type a time or drag the slider. Then keep it on your desktop.

Today

Type a time in any city

The converter sits in the top bar and reads like a sentence: “If it’s [time] in [city]”.

  1. Type the time. In the top bar, type a time such as 3pm, 9:30 or 15h30.
  2. Pick the city. Choose the city that time belongs to. Every card converts, with +1 day or -1 day where the date changes.
  3. Share it. Select Copy times to put every converted time on the clipboard, then Back to now to return to the current time.

Or scrub through the day

No typing at all: scroll over the clocks and watch every city move together.

  • Mouse wheel

    Turn the wheel over any card and every clock moves in 15-minute steps. One notch is one step, at any display scaling.

  • Day line

    Drag along the line at the bottom of a card to slide through that city’s day.

  • Keyboard

    [ and ] move 15 minutes, Page Up and Page Down an hour.

  • Back to now

    One button returns every clock to the current time.

Midnight, other days and the new year

When a city is on a different day, its card says +1 day or -1 day. The end of the year is no different: 17:00 on 31 December 2026 in San Francisco is 01:00 on 1 January 2027 in London and 14:00 in Auckland, both +1 day.

To convert a time on another day, use the date shortcuts: Today, Tomorrow, then the next five days by name. They count from the source city’s calendar, so “Tomorrow” means tomorrow in the city you typed the time for.

Daylight saving time, done right

Most conversion mistakes happen around clock changes. Each conversion uses the rules for its own date, so these come out right:

  • The weeks when offsets shift

    Europe turns its clocks back on 25 October 2026 and the US on 1 November. In between, 9:00 in New York is 13:00 in London, not 14:00.

  • Times that don’t exist

    On 14 March 2027, New York jumps from 02:00 to 03:00. Type 2:30 for that day and the app moves it forward by the gap, to 03:30, the same way Windows does.

  • Times that happen twice

    On 1 November 2026, 01:30 comes round twice in New York. The app takes the first one, 01:30 EDT, which is 05:30 in London.

  • A note before it happens

    In the week before a change, the card says so, for example “Clocks +1h tomorrow”. Hover the note for the exact date and time.

See who’s likely asleep

While you’re converting, any city where it’s between 22:00 and 07:00 dims and shows a moon. It’s a quick way to spot the teammate you’d be calling at 05:30.

The card’s day and night colors still follow the real sun. Lisbon at 21:30 in late September is already dark (the sun set at 19:33), but it only gets the moon from 22:00.
